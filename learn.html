A ket represents a quantum state.

Example: \( |\psi\rangle \)

A general quantum state: \( |\psi\rangle = a|0\rangle + b|1\rangle \)

A bra is the conjugate transpose of a ket.

Example: \( \langle \psi| \)

Basis states are the simplest quantum states.

\( |0\rangle = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \)

\( |1\rangle = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \)

The inner product combines a bra and a ket.

Example: \( \langle \phi | \psi \rangle \)

It produces a scalar value.

Quantum states must be normalized.

Condition: \( |a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1 \)

This ensures total probability equals 1.

Operators act on quantum states.

Example: \( \hat{A}|\psi\rangle \)

Operators are represented using matrices.

The outer product combines a ket and a bra.

Example: \( |\psi\rangle \langle \phi| \)

It produces an operator (matrix).

The tensor product combines two quantum states into a larger system.

Example: \( |0\rangle \otimes |1\rangle = |01\rangle \)

Tensor products are used to describe multi-qubit systems.

Quantum gates are operators that act on quantum states.

They are represented using matrices.

Pauli-X Gate (NOT Gate): Swaps \( |0\rangle \) and \( |1\rangle \)

\( \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \)

Pauli-Z Gate: Flips the sign of \( |1\rangle \)

\( \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \)

Hadamard Gate: Creates superposition states.

\( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \)

Measurement determines the outcome of a quantum state.

If a state is: \( |\psi\rangle = a|0\rangle + b|1\rangle \)

The probability of measuring \( |0\rangle \) is: \( P(0) = |a|^2 \)

The probability of measuring \( |1\rangle \) is: \( P(1) = |b|^2 \)

The total probability must always equal 1.
